Our Origin

Phakama Eastern Cape was born out of a profound realization: the people of this province can no longer wait for top-down political salvation.

Despite its rich history and immense potential, the Eastern Cape has been held back by systemic failures, lack of accountability, and a disconnect between governance and community needs.

We are a developmental movement focused on immediate, pragmatic solutions and rigorous accountability.

About the Initiative

A Bank Built by the Community, for the Community

Phakama Eastern Cape is working to establish a Community Financial Institution (CFI) — a member-owned, democratically governed financial cooperative that will give Eastern Cape communities direct access to savings, credit, and financial services that mainstream banks have historically failed to provide.

Rooted in the co-operative principles of shared ownership and mutual benefit, the Phakama EC CFI will be built from the ground up by community members, for community members.

This initiative is a direct response to the reality faced by millions of South Africans — particularly in rural and semi-urban areas of the Eastern Cape — who remain excluded from formal financial systems.

Why It Matters

Financial Exclusion Is a Structural Problem

~30%
of South Africans remain unbanked or underbanked

Millions of people — disproportionately in the Eastern Cape — have no access to a formal bank account.

High Cost
of commercial banking excludes low-income households

Monthly fees, minimum balance requirements, and complex product structures make mainstream banking unaffordable.

Predatory
lenders fill the gap at enormous cost

In the absence of responsible alternatives, communities turn to microlenders and loan sharks charging exorbitant interest rates.

R billions
in community wealth leaves local economies annually

Money earned in under-served communities is spent through institutions with no local accountability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What You Need to Know

A CFI is a member-owned, community-controlled financial cooperative that provides savings and credit services to its members. Unlike commercial banks, profits stay within the community and decisions are made democratically by members.
All members of the Phakama EC Civic Movement are eligible to join. Membership is open to individuals, households, and small businesses in the Eastern Cape who share the movement's values of community upliftment and financial inclusion.
Founding membership requires contributing to the CFI's initial share capital via the dedicated Stokvel Account. Contact us at info@phakamaec.org.za or call +27 79 920 0361 to receive the full onboarding guide.
The CFI will be registered with and regulated by the Prudential Authority under the Co-operative Banks Development Agency (CBDA) framework — the same regulatory environment that governs all registered co-operative financial institutions in South Africa.
Initially, the CFI will offer member savings accounts and micro-loans. Over time, the range of services will expand based on member needs and regulatory permissions.
Contributions made during this development phase are held in the dedicated Stokvel Account and will form part of the CFI's founding share capital upon registration. All transactions are recorded and transparent to members.

Phakama EC Leadership Explores Partnership to Revive Community Development Centre

Senior leadership of Phakama Eastern Cape visited a community development centre to explore a potential partnership that could breathe new life into a facility once at the heart of local economic activity.

In a significant step towards grassroots economic revitalisation, the leadership of Phakama Eastern Cape recently conducted a visit to a community development centre with the aim of exploring a meaningful partnership to restore the facility to its former standing as a hub of community activity and empowerment.

The centre, which was once a thriving nucleus of food production and skills training for residents in the surrounding area, has in recent years fallen into a state of disuse — a reflection of the broader challenges facing rural and semi-urban communities across the Eastern Cape.

The visit by Phakama EC's leadership signals a shared commitment to reversing this decline. Representatives engaged directly with community stakeholders, toured the grounds, and held substantive discussions around the feasibility of establishing a formal partnership.

"We believe that spaces like this are not just buildings — they are the infrastructure of hope," said a Phakama EC representative following the visit. "When a community has a place to gather, to learn, to grow food and develop skills, it becomes capable of charting its own future."

Central to the proposed partnership is a vision for the centre to once again serve as a centre of agricultural production — providing community members with the tools, training, and land access needed to grow their own produce and build food security from the ground up.

The visit also highlights Phakama EC's broader strategic focus on community-driven development — a recognition that lasting change must be rooted in the strength and agency of communities themselves.

Discussions are ongoing, and further engagements with relevant stakeholders, including local government and community representatives, are expected in the coming weeks.

Phakama EC Holds Peaceful March Calling for Stronger Immigration Enforcement

Phakama Eastern Cape took to the streets in a peaceful, lawful march to raise community concerns about illegal immigration and to call on government to strengthen the enforcement of South Africa's immigration laws.

In a clear demonstration of its commitment to civic participation and constructive engagement, Phakama Eastern Cape organised and led a peaceful march through the streets of the Eastern Cape, drawing together community members united by a shared concern over the impact of illegal immigration on local communities.

Participants carried placards and banners calling for lawful immigration, effective border management, and greater community safety — engaging with members of the public along the route in a spirit of dialogue and civic awareness.

The march was conducted entirely within the bounds of the law and proceeded peacefully from start to finish. Phakama EC emphasised that the action was not directed against any individual or group of people, but was a legitimate exercise in civic expression.

"We are a law-abiding movement and we believe in peaceful, constructive action as the most effective path to change," said a Phakama EC spokesperson. "This march was about holding government accountable."

Marchers advocated for three core priorities: lawful immigration processes that respect both South African residents and those seeking to enter the country through proper channels; effective border management to prevent illegal crossings; and enhanced community safety measures.

The June march was one of a series of civic actions undertaken by Phakama EC as part of its broader mission to champion the interests of Eastern Cape communities and to hold all spheres of government accountable to the people they serve.

Community Action 30 June 2025

Eastern Cape Communities March Against Illegal Immigration

Hundreds of Eastern Cape residents took to the streets on 30 June in a peaceful but powerful march calling on government to enforce immigration laws and protect local jobs, services, and communities from the strain of unregulated migration.

Organised by Phakama Eastern Cape alongside community organisations and residents from across the province, the march drew participants from multiple wards who voiced their frustration over illegal immigration, unemployment, and the failure of authorities to act.

Marchers carried South African flags and placards as they moved through the streets, demanding that government prioritise South African citizens in the allocation of jobs, housing, and public services.

"We are not against foreigners — we are against illegality. Our communities are suffering and we need our government to act," said one of the march organisers.

A memorandum of demands was submitted to relevant authorities calling for stricter enforcement of immigration policy, increased border control, and greater accountability from local government.

Phakama Eastern Cape reiterated its commitment to peaceful, lawful civic action as the most powerful tool available to citizens who want real change.
